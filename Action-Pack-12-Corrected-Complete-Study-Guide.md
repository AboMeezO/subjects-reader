# Action Pack 12 - Detailed Grammar Summary

This is the corrected version. Each grammar point includes use, structure, clues, examples, and common mistakes. It follows the grammar sequence from the Student's Book and the Activity Book grammar notes.

## Unit 1 - Present Simple

**Use:** Present facts, general truths, routines, habits, and fixed/scheduled future events.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + base verb / verb+s/es`

- `Negative: Subject + do/does not + base verb`

- `Question: Do/Does + subject + base verb?`

**Common clues:** always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays, generally, facts, timetables

**Examples:**

- Students use computers at school.

- The Earth goes round the Sun.

- The train leaves at eight tomorrow morning.

- She does not use social media at night.

- Do you study English every day?

**Watch out:**

- Do not add s after does: She does not uses -> She does not use.

- Use the base verb after do/does.

## Unit 1 - Present Continuous

**Use:** Actions happening now, temporary situations, repeated present actions with always, and arranged future plans.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing`

- `Negative: Subject + am/is/are not + verb-ing`

- `Question: Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?`

**Common clues:** now, at the moment, currently, today, this week, always for repeated annoying actions, future arrangement context

**Examples:**

- I am writing an email now.

- She is staying in Irbid for a few months.

- They are always asking the same question.

- We are meeting our teacher tomorrow.

- Are you using the tablet now?

**Watch out:**

- Do not use it for permanent facts: I am living in Jordan forever -> I live in Jordan.

- Remember be before verb-ing.

## Unit 1 - Present Perfect

**Use:** Past action connected to now: life experience, recent result, unfinished time period, or a state continuing until now.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + have/has + past participle`

- `Negative: Subject + have/has not + past participle`

- `Question: Have/Has + subject + past participle?`

**Common clues:** already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for, today, this week, recently, so far

**Examples:**

- I have finished my homework.

- She has lived in Amman for six years.

- Have you ever used a smart watch?

- They have not completed the project yet.

- He has lost his keys, so he cannot enter the house.

**Watch out:**

- Do not use finished past markers with it: I have visited Aqaba yesterday -> I visited Aqaba yesterday.

- Use has with he/she/it.

## Unit 1 - Present Perfect Continuous

**Use:** An action that started in the past and continues now, repeated activity until now, or a recently finished long action with a visible result.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + have/has been + verb-ing`

- `Negative: Subject + have/has not been + verb-ing`

- `Question: Have/Has + subject + been + verb-ing?`

**Common clues:** since, for, all day, lately, recently, how long

**Examples:**

- I have been studying English for two hours.

- She has been working on the website since morning.

- They have been using tablets in class recently.

- Have you been revising all day?

- He is tired because he has been running.

**Watch out:**

- Use since for starting point and for for duration.

- Do not remove been: I have studying -> I have been studying.

## Unit 1 - Past Simple

**Use:** Finished past actions, past routines, and past states over a completed time period.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + past verb`

- `Negative: Subject + did not + base verb`

- `Question: Did + subject + base verb?`

**Common clues:** yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010, when I was young, once, then

**Examples:**

- The first smartphone appeared in 2007.

- I visited the museum yesterday.

- They did not buy a new laptop last year.

- Did you watch the programme last night?

- When I was younger, I played computer games every day.

**Watch out:**

- After did, use base verb: Did you went? -> Did you go?

- Regular verbs take -ed, but many common verbs are irregular.

## Unit 1 - Past Continuous

**Use:** An action in progress around a past moment, background action interrupted by another action, or a long activity in the past.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + was/were + verb-ing`

- `Negative: Subject + was/were not + verb-ing`

- `Question: Was/Were + subject + verb-ing?`

**Common clues:** while, when, at 7 p.m. yesterday, all morning, as

**Examples:**

- I was writing an email when the laptop switched off.

- They were studying while their father was working.

- At nine o’clock, she was preparing her presentation.

- Were you using the computer when I called?

- He was not listening during the lesson.

**Watch out:**

- Use past simple for the interrupting action: I was walking when it rained started -> when it started to rain.

## Unit 1 - Past Perfect

**Use:** The earlier action before another past action or specific past moment.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + had + past participle`

- `Negative: Subject + had not + past participle`

- `Question: Had + subject + past participle?`

**Common clues:** before, after, by the time, already, when + later past action

**Examples:**

- Mohammad had checked his emails before he started work.

- By the time we arrived, the lesson had begun.

- She had not saved the file, so she lost her work.

- Had they finished the project before the deadline?

- I understood the problem after the teacher had explained it.

**Watch out:**

- Use it only when you need to show which past action happened first.

---

## Unit 1 - Future with will

**Use:** Predictions without clear evidence, spontaneous decisions, promises, hopes, and opinions about the future.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + will + base verb`

- `Negative: Subject + will not/won’t + base verb`

- `Question: Will + subject + base verb?`

**Common clues:** I think, I hope, probably, maybe, perhaps, one day, future prediction without evidence

**Examples:**

- I think technology will change education.

- Maybe people will use smaller computers in the future.

- I will help you with your homework.

- They won’t finish the report tonight.

- Will students use books in fifty years?

**Watch out:**

- Do not use will + to: will to study -> will study.

---

## Unit 1 - Future with going to

**Use:** Future plans/intentions and predictions based on present evidence.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + am/is/are going to + base verb`

- `Negative: Subject + am/is/are not going to + base verb`

- `Question: Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + base verb?`

**Common clues:** plan, intention, look at..., evidence now, already decided

**Examples:**

- I am going to create a school website.

- Look at the clouds. It is going to rain.

- They are not going to buy a new tablet this month.

- Are you going to study medicine?

- She is going to present her project tomorrow.

**Watch out:**

- Use be before going to.

---

## Unit 1 - Present Simple Passive

**Use:** When the action is more important than the doer in the present.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + am/is/are + past participle`

- `Optional agent: by + doer`

**Common clues:** processes, facts, general statements, unknown/unimportant doer

**Examples:**

- Smartphones are sold around the world.

- English is spoken in many countries.

- The website is updated every week.

- The files are not saved automatically.

- Is Arabic taught at your school?

**Watch out:**

- Passive needs be + past participle: Smartphones sold -> Smartphones are sold.

---

## Unit 1 - Past Simple Passive

**Use:** When the action is more important than the doer in the past.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + was/were + past participle`

- `Optional agent: by + doer`

**Common clues:** past inventions, historical facts, completed past actions

**Examples:**

- The first PC was produced in 1974.

- The computer chip was developed in 1958.

- The emails were sent yesterday.

- The old building was not repaired.

- When was the first smartphone invented?

**Watch out:**

- Use was/were according to the subject, not the agent.

## Unit 1 - Reported Speech

**Use:** Reporting what someone said without quoting the exact words. Tenses usually move one step back. Pronouns and time words often change.

**Structure:**

- `Reporting clause + that + reported clause`

- `said + statement`

- `told + object + statement`

**Common clues:** said, told, explained, reported, claimed

**Examples:**

- Direct: “I use a laptop.” -> Reported: He said that he used a laptop.

- Direct: “I am studying.” -> Reported: She said that she was studying.

- Direct: “I have finished.” -> Reported: He said that he had finished.

- Direct: “I bought a tablet.” -> Reported: She said that she had bought a tablet.

- Direct: “We will help.” -> Reported: They said that they would help.

**Watch out:**

- Do not use told without an object: He told that... -> He said that... / He told me that...

- Past perfect usually stays past perfect.

## Unit 2 - be used to

**Use:** Something is familiar or normal for someone now.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + am/is/are + used to + noun/pronoun/verb-ing`

- `Past: Subject + was/were + used to + noun/verb-ing`

**Common clues:** familiar now, accustomed to, no longer strange

**Examples:**

- I am used to studying late.

- She is used to speaking English every day.

- They are used to the traffic in the city.

- He was not used to getting up early.

- Are you used to online learning?

**Watch out:**

- After be used to, use verb-ing, not infinitive: used to study -> used to studying.

---

## Unit 2 - used to

**Use:** A past habit or past state that is not true now.

**Structure:**

- `Affirmative: Subject + used to + base verb`

- `Negative: Subject + did not use to + base verb`

- `Question: Did + subject + use to + base verb?`

**Common clues:** in the past, when I was younger, but now, these days

**Examples:**

- I used to watch cartoons, but now I watch documentaries.

- She used to be a teacher, but now she is retired.

- They did not use to have smartphones.

- Did you use to play outside every day?

- My father used to walk to school.

**Watch out:**

- In questions/negatives, use use to after did: Did you used to -> Did you use to.

## Unit 2 - Past Perfect Continuous

**Use:** A longer action that had been happening up to a specific past moment. Often explains a past result.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + had been + verb-ing`

**Common clues:** for, since, by the time, before, looked tired/hot/wet because

**Examples:**

- I had been running for half an hour when I saw you.

- By the time the bus arrived, we had been waiting for an hour.

- She was tired because she had been cooking all afternoon.

- Had you been studying before your friend called?

- They had not been sleeping well before the exam.

**Watch out:**

- Past perfect continuous focuses on duration before a past point; past perfect simple focuses on completion.

---

## Unit 3 - Future Continuous

**Use:** An action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + will be + verb-ing`

**Common clues:** this time tomorrow, at 8 p.m. next week, in ten years’ time, during future time

**Examples:**

- This time next year, I will be preparing for my final exams.

- At eight tonight, she will be studying biology.

- What will you be doing in ten years?

- They will not be working during the holiday.

- Will you be attending the lecture tomorrow?

**Watch out:**

- Use will be + verb-ing, not will + verb-ing.

---

## Unit 3 - Future Perfect

**Use:** An action completed before a specific future time.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + will have + past participle`

**Common clues:** by, by the time, before + future point, by 2030

**Examples:**

- By 2030, scientists will have developed better medical robots.

- By the time we arrive, the train will have left.

- She will have finished the report before noon.

- They will not have completed the project by Monday.

- Will you have graduated by next year?

**Watch out:**

- Use by + future deadline, not at + duration.

## Unit 4 - Cleft Sentences

**Use:** Emphasizing one part of the sentence: person, thing, time, place, or reason.

**Structure:**

- `The person who + clause + be + emphasized person`

- `The thing that + clause + be + emphasized thing`

- `The place where + clause + be + emphasized place`

- `It + be + emphasized part + that/who + clause`

**Common clues:** emphasis, correction, focus on one part of information

**Examples:**

- Huda won the prize last year. -> The person who won the prize last year was Huda.

- Huda won the art prize. -> The prize that Huda won was the art prize.

- The Olympic Games were held in London in 2012. -> It was in London that the Olympic Games were held in 2012.

- I like the way he explains ideas. -> The thing that I like is the way he explains ideas.

- The place where we met was the library.

**Watch out:**

- Do not change the original meaning; only move the emphasized information.

---

## Unit 4 - Defining Relative Clauses

**Use:** Identifying exactly which person, thing, place, or time we mean. Essential information.

**Structure:**

- `Person: noun + who/that + clause`

- `Thing/animal: noun + which/that + clause`

- `Place: noun + where + clause`

- `Time: noun + when + clause`

**Common clues:** necessary information, no commas

**Examples:**

- The scientist who invented this device was famous.

- The book that I borrowed is useful.

- This is the school where I studied.

- Friday is the day when we usually meet.

- The person I called did not answer.

**Watch out:**

- Do not use commas for defining clauses.

- The relative pronoun can sometimes be omitted when it is the object, not the subject.

---

## Unit 4 - Non-defining Relative Clauses

**Use:** Adding extra information about a known noun. The sentence still makes sense without it.

**Structure:**

- `Known noun, who/which/where/when + extra information, rest of sentence`

**Common clues:** extra information, commas, proper names

**Examples:**

- Jabir ibn Hayyan, who was a famous scientist, contributed to chemistry.

- The Sahara Desert, which is in Africa, is very hot.

- Amman, where many students live, has several universities.

- The exam, which was difficult, lasted two hours.

- Our teacher, who speaks English well, helped us.

**Watch out:**

- Do not omit the relative pronoun in non-defining clauses.

- Use commas around the extra information.

---

## Unit 5 - Articles: a/an, the, zero article

**Use:** Choosing whether a noun needs an indefinite article, definite article, or no article.

**Structure:**

- `a/an + singular countable noun mentioned for the first time`

- `the + known/specific/unique noun or superlative`

- `zero article + general plural/uncountable nouns, most countries/languages/continents/days/months/years`

**Common clues:** first mention vs known thing, general vs specific, unique objects, superlatives

**Examples:**

- I saw a student in the library. The student was reading a book.

- The Sun gives us light.

- Children usually like sweets.

- Arabic is spoken in Jordan.

- The longest river in Egypt is the Nile.

**Watch out:**

- Do not use a/an with plural or uncountable nouns: a information -> information.

- Use the when the listener knows exactly which thing you mean.

---

## Unit 5 - British English vs American English

**Use:** Recognizing common grammar and spelling differences between British and American English.

**Structure:**

- `BE often uses present perfect where AE may use past simple`

- `AE: gotten; BE: got`

- `AE: have for possession; BE: have got`

**Common clues:** yet/already with past simple in AE, spelling differences like color/colour, center/centre

**Examples:**

- BE: Have you seen that film yet? / AE: Did you see that film yet?

- BE: He has got a sister. / AE: He has a sister.

- BE: colour, centre, favourite. / AE: color, center, favorite.

- BE: He had got some ice cream. / AE: He had gotten some ice cream.

**Watch out:**

- Do not mix spelling systems randomly inside formal writing.

## Unit 6 - Quantifiers and Comparisons

**Use:** Comparing adjectives, adverbs, quantities, and numbers.

**Structure:**

- `more/less + adjective/adverb + than`

- `as + adjective/adverb + as`

- `the most/least + adjective/adverb`

- `as much + uncountable noun + as`

- `as many + plural countable noun + as`

**Common clues:** compare two things, compare quantities, most/least among a group

**Examples:**

- Science is more interesting than history.

- Maths is not as popular as English.

- This course is the most useful option.

- There are not as many students in our class as in yours.

- I do not eat as much fast food as my brother.

**Watch out:**

- Use many with countable plural nouns and much with uncountable nouns.

---

## Unit 7 - Indirect Questions

**Use:** Polite or formal questions using statement word order.

**Structure:**

- `Question opener + wh-word + subject + verb?`

- `Question opener + if/whether + subject + verb?`

- `Openers: Could you tell me..., Do you know..., Do you mind telling me..., Could you explain...`

**Common clues:** polite request, formal situation, not direct question order

**Examples:**

- Direct: Where is the library? -> Could you tell me where the library is?

- Direct: Is there a bank near here? -> Do you know if there is a bank near here?

- Direct: Why is the train late? -> Do you mind telling me why the train is late?

- Direct: How did you solve it? -> Could you explain how you solved it?

**Watch out:**

- Do not use question word order after the opener: Do you know where is the library? -> where the library is.

---

## Unit 7 - Impersonal Passive

**Use:** Formal reporting of beliefs, claims, thoughts, and opinions. Common in academic/report writing.

**Structure:**

- `It + passive reporting verb + that + clause`

- `Subject + passive reporting verb + to + infinitive`

- `Reporting verbs: said, thought, believed, claimed, known`

**Common clues:** formal tone, people say/think/believe/claim/know

**Examples:**

- People believe that dolphins are intelligent. -> It is believed that dolphins are intelligent.

- They say that the story is true. -> The story is said to be true.

- People know that he is talented. -> He is known to be talented.

- They claim that the method works. -> It is claimed that the method works.

**Watch out:**

- Match infinitive form to time: is believed to be / is believed to have been.

---

## Unit 8 - Phrasal Verbs

**Use:** A verb plus one or two particles with a specific meaning. Some take objects, some do not, and some are separable.

**Structure:**

- `Verb + particle`

- `Verb + particle + object`

- `Verb + object + particle for separable verbs`

- `Verb + particle + particle + object for three-part phrasal verbs`

**Common clues:** carry out, look into, come up with, grow up, get on, point out

**Examples:**

- The candidates carried out a short task.

- We are looking into the problem.

- She grew up in Aqaba.

- Please point the mistake out. / Please point it out.

- They came up with a good idea.

**Watch out:**

- Object pronouns go before the particle in separable phrasal verbs: point it out, not point out it.

---

## Unit 8 - Question Tags

**Use:** Checking or confirming information after a statement.

**Structure:**

- `Positive statement + negative tag`

- `Negative statement + positive tag`

- `Use the same auxiliary/modal; if there is no auxiliary, use do/does/did`

- `Let’s + verb, shall we?`

**Common clues:** ..., isn’t it? ..., do you? ..., haven’t they? ..., shall we?

**Examples:**

- You have read this book, haven’t you?

- She does not speak French, does she?

- They can help us, can’t they?

- He lives in Wadi Musa, doesn’t he?

- Let’s go to the library, shall we?

**Watch out:**

- Match tense and auxiliary. Do not use “isn’t it” for every sentence.

---

## Unit 8 - Passive Forms Revision

**Use:** When the subject receives the action or the doer is unknown/unimportant.

**Structure:**

- `Basic passive: subject + be + past participle`

- `Present simple: am/is/are + past participle`

- `Past simple: was/were + past participle`

- `Present perfect: have/has been + past participle`

- `Future: will be + past participle`

- `Modal: modal + be + past participle`

**Common clues:** by + agent, official/formal style, processes, reports, inventions

**Examples:**

- The competition is held every year.

- Many important things were invented in the twentieth century.

- The school has been renovated recently.

- The project will be completed before the deadline.

- This problem can be solved easily.

**Watch out:**

- The tense is shown by be, not by the main verb. Main verb must be past participle.

---

## Unit 9 - Wish / If only for Past Regrets

**Use:** Regret about something that happened or did not happen in the past.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + wish/if only + subject + had/hadn’t + past participle`

**Common clues:** regret, past mistake, too late to change, should have feeling

**Examples:**

- I wish I had studied harder for the exam.

- If only we had caught the earlier bus.

- She wishes she had not bought those shoes.

- I wish I had saved the file before the computer crashed.

**Watch out:**

- Use past perfect after wish for past regrets, not past simple.

---

## Unit 9 - Wish / If only for Present Wishes

**Use:** Wishes about the present that are impossible or unlikely now.

**Structure:**

- `Subject + wish/if only + subject + past simple`

- `With be: was/were; were is common in formal English`

**Common clues:** present dissatisfaction, impossible/unlikely now

**Examples:**

- I wish I knew the answer.

- If only we lived in a bigger flat.

- He wishes he were taller.

- I wish I had more free time.

**Watch out:**

- Although the meaning is present, the form after wish is past simple.

---

## Unit 10 - Zero Conditional

**Use:** Facts, rules, and inevitable results.

**Structure:**

- `If + present simple, present simple`

- `Present simple + if + present simple`

**Common clues:** always true, scientific facts, general results

**Examples:**

- If plants do not get enough sunlight, they die.

- Water turns to ice if the temperature falls below zero.

- If students revise regularly, they remember more.

- If you press this button, the screen turns on.

**Watch out:**

- Do not use will in the result for zero conditional facts.

---

## Unit 10 - First Conditional

**Use:** Real possible future situations and their likely results.

**Structure:**

- `If + present simple, will + base verb`

- `will + base verb + if + present simple`

- `Alternatives to if: provided that, as long as, unless, when, even if`

**Common clues:** real future possibility, promise, warning, plan result

**Examples:**

- If you get an interview, you will need to prepare well.

- I will buy the book if it is not too expensive.

- Unless you revise, you will forget the rules.

- Even if the course is difficult, I will continue.

- When I finish school, I will apply for university.

**Watch out:**

- Do not use will in the if-clause: If I will pass -> If I pass.

---

## Unit 10 - Third Conditional

**Use:** Imaginary past situation and imaginary past result; something did not happen.

**Structure:**

- `If + past perfect, would have + past participle`

- `would have + past participle + if + past perfect`

**Common clues:** past regret, impossible past, imaginary result

**Examples:**

- If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.

- I would not have gone to the library if my friend had not invited me.

- If we had left earlier, we would have arrived on time.

- She would have got the job if she had prepared better.

**Watch out:**

- Both parts refer to the past, but the structure is past perfect + would have.

---

## Unit 10 - Third Conditional with could/might

**Use:** Imaginary past result with less certainty or ability/possibility.

**Structure:**

- `If + past perfect, could/might have + past participle`

**Common clues:** possible but not certain result, ability in imaginary past

**Examples:**

- If I had prepared better, I might have won first prize.

- If I had slept well, I could have concentrated better.

- They might have become champions if they had trained harder.

- If I had chosen another school, I might not have studied French.

**Watch out:**

- Use might have/could have when the result is possible, not certain.

---

## Unit 10 - Second Conditional Revision

**Use:** Imaginary or unlikely present/future situations.

**Structure:**

- `If + past simple, would + base verb`

- `would + base verb + if + past simple`

**Common clues:** imaginary now, unlikely future, advice with if I were you

**Examples:**

- If I were in London, I would visit the British Museum.

- If I had more time, I would learn another language.

- She would apply for the job if she had enough experience.

- If I were you, I would revise every day.

**Watch out:**

- Use past simple after if, but the meaning is present/future.

# Action Pack 12 - Writing Guide

## Discursive essay
Discuss both sides. Use contrast and consequence linkers: however, whereas, on the one hand, on the other hand, therefore, as a result.

## Report
Use factual tone, title, introduction, findings, conclusion/recommendations. Useful openings: The aim of this report is to..., This report examines...

## Descriptive essay
Describe a person, place, invention, future situation, or event with organized details. Use clear paragraphs and precise adjectives.

## Summary
Keep main ideas only. Remove examples, repeated ideas, and minor details. Do not add opinion.

## Argumentative essay
Take a clear position. Give reasons, examples, counterargument, and conclusion. Arrange arguments logically.

## Blog post
More personal than an essay, but still organized. Use a clear topic, personal opinion, and direct reader-friendly style.

## Persuasive letter
State the problem, explain consequences, propose solution, and request action politely.

## Competition entry
Answer the prompt exactly. Use engaging language and clear reasons.

## Review
Describe the event/place/book/service, evaluate positives and negatives, then give a recommendation.

## CV and covering letter
CV: personal details, education, experience, skills, references. Covering letter: job applied for, why you fit, relevant experience, polite closing.

# Action Pack 12 - Exercise Strategy Guide

## Verb-form questions
1. Underline the time clue.
2. Decide present/past/future.
3. Decide simple/continuous/perfect/passive.
4. Check subject agreement.

## Rewrite questions
1. Keep the exact meaning.
2. Identify the requested grammar clue.
3. Change only the grammar structure.
4. Check tense, pronouns, articles, and word order.

## Reading comprehension
1. Skim title and pictures.
2. Read questions before detailed reading.
3. Underline keywords.
4. Find synonyms in the text.
5. Answer from the text, not from memory.

## Writing tasks
1. Identify the required writing type.
2. Make a paragraph plan.
3. Use unit vocabulary.
4. Include the target grammar where natural.
5. Check grammar, spelling, punctuation, and paragraphing.
